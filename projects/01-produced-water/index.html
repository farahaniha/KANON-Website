The Problem: Oil & gas treatment facilities face a silent threat. Equipment failures and high-load events can cause environmental breaches before operators notice. The data is noisy, alerts are frequent, and the cost of a missed failure is catastrophic.

The Solution: We built a synthetic lab to simulate 6,570 daily measurements (730 facility-days) across two facilities. We planted known events (an April high-load, an August equipment failure) and challenged our analytics to find them using only the observed data.

The Result: Our statistical detector successfully identified both real elevation events with a 3–4 day latency, and we honestly documented the 2 false alarms (which occurred during a sustained February fluctuation).

1. Business Impact (Illustrative)

Note: The following figures are illustrative, not measured field data.

For a facility processing roughly 100 alerts per year, a precision of 60.0% translates to approximately 40 false alarms annually. Assuming a manual inspection cost of $500 per alert, the annual false-alarm cost is roughly $20,000. This is significantly lower than the cost of a single missed equipment failure (estimated at $50,000+).

Interpretation: A precision of 60.0% means that roughly 2 out of every 5 alerts are false. However, in a screening context, inspecting 40 false alarms per year is cheap ($20,000) compared to missing a single catastrophic failure ($50,000+). The system is financially viable.

3. Methodology

3.1 Detection Algorithm

We apply a 7-day rolling median to the daily effluent oil & grease measurements. A day is flagged as an anomaly if the rolling median exceeds the facility's full-year baseline by both a statistically significant margin (2 standard deviations) and a practical margin (50% increase).

Interpretation: The 7-day rolling median smooths out daily sensor noise. The dual threshold (2-sigma AND 50%) ensures we only flag events that are both statistically unusual and practically significant to operators.

6.2 Uncertainty & Sensitivity

Because we only have 3 true elevation events, the 95% Wilson Confidence Intervals are very wide:

  • Event Precision: 23.1% – 88.2%
  • Event Recall: 43.8% – 100.0%

Why the uncertainty chart shows baseline as the biggest risk: Because using a full-year median includes the April high-load event in the baseline calculation, which slightly inflates the mean and lowers sensitivity. A causal (trailing) baseline would be more sensitive but would miss the first few days of any new event.

6.3 Limitations Register

  • L1: The February false alarm is a sustained anomaly. It lasted 6 days, which is longer than pure daily noise. It is likely an artifact of the synthetic AR(1) process.
  • L2: The algorithm detects elevations (increases above baseline). It is structurally incapable of detecting a recovery (return to baseline).
  • L3: The business impact section assumes 100 alerts/year and $500/inspection. These are illustrative, not measured.
  • L4: The "naive rule" was a simple threshold of 50% above baseline, with no statistical threshold (2-sigma) and no event merging.
